Hello, and welcome to my personal website! My name is Timothy, and I'm a data scientist on [Stanford Health Care's Technology & Digital Solutions (TDS)](https://stanfordhealthcare.org/) Data Science team. This site is a brief introduction to me and the work I do across medicine, machine learning, and clinical AI. You can learn more about my background on my **[CV](cv.html)** and browse my **[publications](publications.html)**.

I originally came to Stanford as an [MD/PhD student](https://med.stanford.edu/mstp.html) and recently completed my PhD in computational cancer biology. My graduate work focused on applying machine learning to [high-dimensional single-cell data in pediatric leukemia](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cyto.a.24158), with the goal of identifying disease-associated cell populations and improving our understanding of patient outcomes. Today, my work sits at the intersection of applied machine learning, clinical operations, and responsible AI governance.

A central focus of my current work is Stanford Health Care's LLM-powered clinical AI platform, **[ChatEHR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00074)**. I contribute across the ecosystem: evaluating model behavior, designing clinician-facing experiences, developing agentic workflows for chart review and abstraction, and helping translate technical systems into tools that can operate safely in real clinical settings. I have helped build and evaluate automations for tasks such as identifying patients who may benefit from specialty consults, [screening surgical patients for elevated perioperative risk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17234), supporting [tumor board preparation](https://news.microsoft.com/source/features/ai/meet-4-developers-leading-the-way-with-ai-agents/), and detecting potential post-operative surgical-site infections.

I am especially interested in the practical work of making AI useful in health care: deciding which clinical problems are worth solving, designing systems that fit into real workflows, measuring whether they are safe and effective, and building governance structures that allow them to improve over time. Much of my work focuses on the space between model development and clinical impact: [evaluation, monitoring, implementation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09048), and the human-centered design choices that determine whether an AI system actually helps the people it is meant to support.

I believe the future of medicine needs people who move fluently between clinical reasoning, technical development, and operational reality — and I'm working to be one of them. My goal is to help build AI systems that are data-driven, patient-focused, transparent about their limitations, and grounded in the needs of clinicians and patients. If you have ideas about how to do that, feel free to [contact me](mailto:tkeyes@stanford.edu).
